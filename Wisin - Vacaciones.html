Vacaciones

Wisin

Chorus
Yo no necesito vacaciones

I don't need vacations

Ni dolores de cabeza

Nor headaches

Solo me bastan mis amigos

My friends are enough for me

The phrase "me basta" means "is enough for me" or "suffices." Wisin uses this line to anchor the central theme of the song: true escapism isn't about expensive trips, but about living in the moment with your close circle (el combo).
Y un traguito de cerveza

And a little sip of beer

"Traguito" is the diminutive form of "trago" (drink/sip). Diminutives are frequently used in Spanish to show affection, relaxedness, or informality—here making the idea of having a quick drink sound casual and pleasant.
Para calmar el estrés, estrés, estrés

To calm down the stress, stress, stress

Que la vida es una sola

Because you only live once

Literally translating to "Because life is only one," this is the classic Spanish expression equivalent to the English idiom YOLO ("You Only Live Once"). It emphasizes seizing the day before time passes.
Y hay que vivirla al derecho y al revés

And you have to live it right side up and upside down

"Al derecho y al revés" is an idiom meaning inside-out, backward and forward, or from every possible angle. It means experiencing life to its fullest extent without holding back.
Verse 1
Llegó el fin de semana, la gente se lo goza

The weekend arrived, people are enjoying it

No te quede' en la casa que la cosa está sabrosa

Don't stay home, things are getting good

Notice the dropped 's' in "quede'" (from quedes), typical of Caribbean Spanish phonetics (Puerto Rico). "La cosa está sabrosa" literally means "the thing is tasty," but colloquially means the atmosphere or party vibe is great.
Prende el motor, pon la música a volar

Start the engine, crank the music up

Que hoy se bebe y hoy se baila hasta el final

Because tonight we drink and tonight we dance until the end

Uses the impersonal "se" structure ("hoy se bebe" = "today drinking happens / we drink"). This construction is widely used in Latin urban music to state a collective expectation or party rule for the night.
